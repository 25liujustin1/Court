# Court — project notes (working title was MADK)

A hidden-identity social deduction card game, inspired by the manga *MADK* (Ryo Suzuri). Players are demons with secret names; knowing a demon's name gives power over it. The noble ranks come from the Ars Goetia's hierarchy.

## Files

- `rules.md` — the rulebook. **Source of truth.** Change rules here first.
- `name-table.tex` — generated by `node sim/name-table.js` from `NAME_LIST` in the playtest page (the official table, settled 2026-11-10 to match the PDF rulebook; Ars Goetia order within each role). The 35 combinations, their demon names, sums and roles (LaTeX).
- `playtest/index.html` — single-file pass-and-play web version used for playtesting. It is published as a claude.ai artifact; it is written as a page fragment (no `<html>`/`<head>`), which the artifact host wraps. It still opens directly in a browser.
- `playtest/test/` — Playwright scripts that drive the page with random actions and assert invariants (`npm test`; `GAMES=20 npm test` for more random games). `regressions.js` covers fixed bugs.
- `playtests/` — notes and data from real games (see its README).
- `sim/` — strategy bots and balance simulation on the real page code (`npm run sim`, see its README).
- `print/` — printable cards and tokens (not built yet).

## Working rules

- When a rule changes: update `rules.md`, then the playtest page, then run the tests and the sim.
- Keep the rulebook plain: short sections, little formatting. Justin prefers rule text he can paste.
- Don't re-propose ideas listed under "Rejected" without a new reason.

## Core numbers (current)

| Thing | Value |
|---|---|
| Starting souls | 2 |
| Income | +2 at the start of your own turn (thrall: produces 2, all to binder; any soul a thrall gets goes to the binder) |
| Devour bonus | +4 souls from the bank per devour, to whoever does it (not for devouring your own thrall; for a paid Marquis devour, the original namer gets it along with the spoils; changed 2026-11-10 from "the payer gets it", which made finishing a marked Marquis free for anyone) |
| Spend cap | none (removed 2026-11-09; was 10 souls per turn) |
| Token purchase | 1 per turn: 1 soul random (was 2 until 2026-11-10: only 8% of buys used it), or 3 souls draw 2 keep 1 (choose by color, unseen) |
| Token rounds | Tokens only buyable in odd rounds until the bag first empties |
| Event card | 1 soul, unlimited |
| Token trade fee | 4 souls to the bank for each side, per token (was 5 until 2026-11-10); passed face down (only the traders know the dots) |
| Marquis devour | 4 souls, not by a Prince |
| Deck (48) | Seize 12, Collect 6, Scrying 6, Ward 9, Pact 3, Meta 2, Arrogation 4, Welfare 6 |
| Roles | sum of dots mod 5: 0 Archduke, 1 Duke, 2 Prince, 3 Marquis, 4 Earl — exactly 3 combinations each |

## Design decisions and why

- **Role = sum mod 5** of 4 symbols with 1–4 dots: the 35 combinations split exactly 7 per role, so roles are equally likely.
- **1–4 dots and 12 Seize cards (adopted 2026-11-09; was 1–3 dots, 15 names, 9 Seize cards).** A role reveal now leaves 7 names instead of 3, so abilities are worth using (Earl went from ~20% to ~23–29% in the sim) and turtling pays less (turtle bots ~38% → ~31%). Naming needs more information (2 tokens leave 10 names, not 6), so Seize cards went from 9 to 12 to keep stalls down; 15 Seize cards added nothing over 12.
- **Using an active ability reveals your role**, which narrows your name from 35 to 7. Hiding is the natural balancer for strong abilities. The Marquis is passive, so it hides best.
- **Naming needs at least 1 of the target's tokens and nobody else holding more.** Makes tokens ammunition, not just information, and stops people sharing memorized tokens to collude.
- **Anti-turtling (added 2026-11-09):** if no other player holds any of a player's tokens (all in their own hand or the bag), anyone may name them. Holding all 4 of your own tokens used to make you un-nameable; the sim showed turtle bots winning most and, once face-down trading let them buy tokens back, 30% of stalled players were un-nameable. Note this also means anyone may name anyone in round 1, while every token is still in the bag (a blind 1-in-35 guess).
- **Wrong naming backfires:** the target chooses bind or devour. Keeps naming a gamble; after a role reveal, players wait for others to guess first.
- **Welfare (2026-11-10): back to the original effect, 6 copies.** The non-thrall with the lowest net worth takes 1 soul from everyone with souls. Thralls are excluded from receiving because a thrall's souls go to their binder. Tried and dropped: richest pays everyone (wiped out small early leads), richest gives half to the poorest (still mostly turtles), split evenly when one player has 2x the souls (adopted briefly; taxes hoarding rather than leading), 2x net worth (a tax on capturing: turtles 33%, Marquis 31%). Welfare is meant to help whoever is behind; accepted that this nudges turtles up (sim at 6 copies: turtles ~27% -> ~31%).
- **Giving away taken tokens (2026-11-10):** after binding or devouring, before looking, the captor may give any of the tokens they took to non-thralls, face down and free (the trade fee exists to stop people pooling known dots; unseen tokens carry no knowledge). A political tool for deals; the bots never use it, so the sim says nothing about it.
- **Ward, Scrying and self-Pacts (2026-11-10, to match the PDF rulebook).** Ward no longer gives an immediate extra turn: it turns a bind/devour (correct naming, paid Marquis devour, broken Pact) into a claim that waits until the end of the target's next turn (more Wards delay again); meanwhile the target can't be named or paid-devoured. The claim passes to whoever captures the claimant; if the target captures the claimant, it ends (rebirth if they'd been named). Scrying gained a second mode: discard 2, look at the top 4, cards that act when drawn take effect, keep 2. A broken Pact with yourself devours you; the Pact names in advance who gets your souls, cards, tokens and thralls. Code: `S.claims`, `offerWard`, `claimsOnCapture`, `resolveClaims`, `ACT.scry2`, `selfPactDevour`. The old Ward turn stack (`S.interrupts`) is kept only so old saves load.
- **Insubordination merged into Arrogation (2026-11-10), 4 copies.** A Duke may keep it; otherwise, if any thrall could use it, the drawer must give it to a thrall (a rebellion); otherwise put it back or discard. Only a Duke can keep it when drawn; a non-Duke who gets one by trade, theft or capture just holds it with no effect (resolving it, or putting it back, would publicly reveal the old holder was a Duke). Showing is mandatory because the usual drawer is a rich binder who would bury it. Sim vs the old two cards: successful rebellions 0.27 -> 0.42 per game, winners who had once been a thrall 8% -> 14%, roles and strategies unchanged. (The old "unusable Insubordination redraws" rule is gone; put back / discard covers it.)
- **Rulebook decisions (2026-11-10):** when you devour, you choose which half of the tokens to take (by color, face down; was random). Thralls can't trade, and nobody can trade with a thrall (they have no souls and almost never cards). Tokens held by thralls count when deciding who holds the most of someone's tokens. A Pact with yourself states its forfeit up front (a share of cards, souls and tokens); breaking it splits that share among the other non-thralls (was: someone binds or devours you).
- **Capture rework (adopted 2026-11-09):** thralls produce 2 souls a turn for their binder (was 1) and any soul a thrall gets goes to the binder; devouring pays 4 souls from the bank. Goal: make acting pay so waiting (turtling) stops dominating. Sim sweep of the bonus with the new bind rules (first capturer wins / turtle edge at 4-6 players): 0 = 39% / 1.27x, 2 = 42% / 1.18x, 3 = 43% / 1.17x, 4 = 43% / 1.11x, 6 = 45% / 1.09x; old rules were 32% / 1.43x. 4 chosen as the knee.
- **Bind vs devour:** bind = all event cards + souls + 1 chosen token + ongoing income; devour = all event cards + souls + half the tokens (rest to the bag). Devouring used to give everything; halving tokens limits snowballing.
- **Token rounds** (odd rounds only until the bag empties) replaced a 4-token holding cap. Both stop the early rush; token rounds also make waiting costly (a skipped window is lost).
- **Selective draws choose by color, dots unseen**, so drawing extra tokens never leaks extra information.
- **Scrying card = draw 3 keep 1 (by color); Collect card = 2 souls.** Makes early event cards worth buying. Since 2026-11-09 the Scrying card only works when tokens can be bought (token rounds) and counts as that turn's 1 token purchase; before, it worked in any round and on top of a purchase.
- **Duke** holds Arrogation to gain the Archduke question (limit: half the other starting players, rounded down, whole game; was half the starting players). The Duke was the weakest role.
- **Arrogation** can be kept by anyone to trade; that also lets players starve the Duke.
- **Marquis** can't be bound; once named it is "marked" and anyone but a Prince can devour it for 4. If a Prince names a Marquis correctly or decides its fate (wrong naming backfire, broken contract), the Prince devours it. This used to leave the Marquis marked for the Prince; the sim showed the Marquis surviving 98% of wrong guesses against Princes, a free shot once a Prince revealed.
- **Unusable Insubordination is discarded, and the drawer draws again for free** (redraw added 2026-11-09: in the sim about 95% of Insubordination draws had no usable thrall, so it was usually a wasted soul; capped at 3 redraws in a row). (changed 2026-11-09 from "shuffle it back into the deck"). The sim found decks that held only Insubordinations: every purchase drew one and put it back, so the deck never emptied and the discard pile (with most Seize cards) never reshuffled. Arrogation could cause the same lock (a deck of only Arrogations put back each time), so it can now also be discarded (2026-11-09); players who want the Seize cards in the discard pile will discard it.
- **Rebirth:** a thrall freed by Insubordination picks any new name; every old token (hands and bag) is swapped 1:1 for a piece of the new name. Their name was public, so they need a clean slate.
- **Token trading allowed but expensive** (4 souls each side; was 5). Traded tokens pass face down: only the two traders know the dots (changed 2026-11-09 from "revealed to all"; the old reveal meant collusion gained nothing private, so watch for two players pooling information on a third). The offer screen shows only colors, so a declined offer leaks nothing.
- **Income on your own turn**, not per round.
- **No spending cap** (removed 2026-11-09, was 10/turn). In the sim, endgame leaders sat on ~34 souls they couldn't turn into Seize cards; the cap and per-turn bot limits stopped wealth from converting into information. `SPEND_CAP` is still a constant (`CFG.SPEND_CAP` in the sim) if a cap needs testing again.

## Rejected (and why)

- Paying 1 soul to steal an event card: same price as the deck but targeted; breaks Ward/Seize sets.
- Seize card + 1 soul variant: kept Seize as is (2 for a card, 4 for a token).
- Draw 3 keep 2 purchase (5 souls): removed; it made collecting your own tokens too easy.
- 4-token holding cap until the bag empties: replaced by token rounds.
- One-token-per-turn limit as a rush fix: didn't bind when income was 1 per round.

## Open questions / watch in playtests

- **Snowballing:** first devour now also takes souls. Watch whether the first player to devour usually wins.
- **Duke** may still be weak if Arrogation gets hoarded; candidates: 3 Arrogations, or a fixed buy-out price.
- **Insubordination failure** gives "all cards and tokens" but not souls; moot now that a thrall's souls go to the binder anyway.
- **Capture rework side effects:** (1) thralls can rarely buy anything, so their turns may feel dead; (2) fixed: a Prince buying from their own thrall now pays the bank (paying the thrall looped the souls straight back, making it free); (3) fixed: no +4 for devouring your own thrall (it allowed an bind-then-devour double dip).
- **Thralls receiving tokens** in trades is allowed for now.
- **Meta card** says "verbally/physically assault"; physical is the designer's call, flagged as a safety risk.
- Unbuilt purchase ideas: Glimpse (5), Recall (6), Scatter (8), Sanctuary (7), Forged Seal (9, with fakes), False Name (10, once per game).

## Playtest page notes

- State lives in one object `S`; actions are `ACT.*` handlers triggered by `data-act` buttons; modals use `ask()`.
- Tunable constants at the top of the script: `SPEND_CAP`, `MARQUIS_KILL`, `TRADE_FEE`, `INCOME`, `DECK_COUNTS`.
- `S.capLifted` means "the bag has been emptied once" (token rounds over). The name is left over from the old token cap.
- Game saves to the browser's localStorage on that device only.
- Ward turns are a stack, `S.interrupts` (they can nest); `normalize()` upgrades old saves that had `S.interrupt`.
- The sim replaces the page's `ask()` and `log()` and calls `ACT.*` directly, so prompt titles are part of the sim's interface: if you reword a prompt title, check `sim/bots.js` `answer()` (unhandled prompts are listed in the sim report).
